# Chapter I: INTRODUCTION

## Background of the Study

A standard fixed-camera illegal parking detection system, when tested in real-world urban traffic, cannot mechanically distinguish a vehicle waiting at an unseen red light from a vehicle that has been illegally parked for an hour. Both produce identical spatial-temporal signatures: they are stationary inside a restricted zone with an expired timer. The root cause of this failure mode is architectural: standard violation decision logic evaluates the candidate vehicle in complete isolation from the dynamic traffic system in which it is embedded.

At the foundational level, automated Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) rely on computer vision architectures to extract spatial data from fixed traffic cameras. The vehicle detection stage utilizes advanced single-stage object detectors such as YOLO to facilitate rapid object recognition [AI Powered Traffic Violations Detection, https://doi.org/10.1109/icmlas67792.2026.11483802]. To maintain persistent vehicle identities across consecutive frames, frameworks integrate multi-object tracking algorithms [Parking Time Violation Tracking Using YOLOv8 and Tracking Algorithms, https://doi.org/10.3390/s23135843], [Real-time Vehicle Detection and Tracking Based on the Combination of YOLOv7 and ByteTrack, https://doi.org/10.54254/2755-2721/4/20230467]. High-speed tracking-by-detection methods, such as Intersection-over-Union (IoU) trackers, have proven highly effective for maintaining track continuity with minimal computational overhead under fixed-camera conditions [High-speed tracking-by-detection without using image information, https://doi.org/10.1109/AVSS.2017.8078516]. 

While vehicle detection and tracking stages are highly robust, the subsequent violation decision logic remains structurally flawed. The conventional method tracks whether a vehicle enters a predefined polygonal Region of Interest (ROI) and increments a frame counter, frequently utilizing a standard temporal threshold of 60 seconds [Parking Violation Detection Using Computer Vision for Urban Traffic Management, https://doi.org/10.1109/iciss63372.2025.11076537], [Detecting illegally parked vehicle based on cumulative dual foreground difference, https://doi.org/10.1109/indin.2016.7819263]. If the elapsed time exceeds this threshold, the system mechanically flags the vehicle as an illegal parking candidate. 

However, evaluating vehicles independently introduces a fundamental vulnerability grounded in traffic flow theory. According to kinematic wave theory, vehicles in an urban corridor are causally coupled; they stop collectively due to upstream conditions, such as traffic signal phase changes or propagating congestion shockwaves [On kinematic waves. II. A theory of traffic flow on long crowded roads, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1955.0089]. A vehicle stopped at a red light is causally coupled to surrounding vehicles by a shared external signal. In contrast, genuine illegal parking is an individual-level anomaly with no causal coupling to surrounding traffic flow. Therefore, a vehicle whose stationary state is temporally decorrelated from the surrounding traffic's collective motion state is theoretically more likely to be a genuine violation. Because standard algorithms ignore this theoretical distinction and evaluate candidates in strict isolation, they inherently misclassify legitimate, causally coupled traffic stops as illegal parking [A New Curb Lane Monitoring and Illegal Parking Impact Estimation Approach Based on Queueing Theory and Computer Vision for Cameras with Low Resolution and Low Frame Rate, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2022.05.024], [iPatrol: Illegal Roadside Parking Detection Leveraging On-road Vehicle Crowdsensing, https://doi.org/10.1109/iwqos61813.2024.10682887], [When Vehicle Crowd-Sensing Meets Complex Driving: Robust Illegal Roadside Parking Detections, https://doi.org/10.1109/tmc.2026.3665356], [Automated Illegal Parking Fine System Using YOLOv8 and PaddleOCR on the Cloud, https://doi.org/10.1109/icscis69190.2026.11453485].

## Gap/Opportunity

Among the fixed-camera frameworks reviewed, strategies for mitigating traffic-induced false positives share a common structural dependency on fixed physical infrastructure, signal states, or static environmental markers visible within the camera frame. Gao et al. [A New Curb Lane Monitoring and Illegal Parking Impact Estimation Approach Based on Queueing Theory and Computer Vision for Cameras with Low Resolution and Low Frame Rate, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2022.05.024] define pedestrian detection zones on crosswalks as proxies for inferring red-light phases. Kathait et al. [Computer Vision and Deep Learning Based Approach for Violations Due to Illegal Parking Detection, https://doi.org/10.5120/ijca2025924506] monitor traffic signal light color states to suspend violation timers during red phases. Makmur et al. [Motorcycle Parking Violation Detection System Using YOLOv7 with Region of Interest Mapping and Object Area Calculation, https://doi.org/10.46604/aiti.2024.14075] and Park et al. [Special Vehicle Classification Algorithm-Based System for Dedicated Parking Zone Violation Detection in South Korea, https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2025.3526862] enforce static Region-of-Interest boundary demarcations on road surfaces. Each of these approaches requires that a specific fixed reference element remains continuously visible and unoccluded within the camera's field of view. When line-of-sight to these elements is compromised by inter-vehicle occlusion, environmental glare, or suboptimal camera placement, the mitigation mechanism degrades or fails entirely [A New Curb Lane Monitoring and Illegal Parking Impact Estimation Approach Based on Queueing Theory and Computer Vision for Cameras with Low Resolution and Low Frame Rate, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2022.05.024], [Illegal Parking Detection Based on Multi-Task Driving Perception, https://doi.org/10.1109/iv55156.2024.10588798], [Real-Time Occlusion-Aware Vehicle Detection and Behavior Analysis Framework for Intelligent Traffic Enforcement, https://doi.org/10.1109/icces67310.2025.11336884].

A separate line of work avoids temporal tracking altogether in favor of semantic spatial co-occurrence. Abella and Catedrilla [Smart Surveillance of Illegal Parking and Littering Detection Using Yolo-Based Machine Learning Algorithms in the Municipality of Los Baños, https://doi.org/10.1109/icaisg68699.2025.11452147] define a violation as the simultaneous detection of a vehicle, a human, and a no-parking sign within a single frame. While the methodology reported a precision of 0.9841 for a littering class, precision for the illegal parking class dropped to 0.7467, yielding 509 combined false positives out of 2,803 total detections [Smart Surveillance of Illegal Parking and Littering Detection Using Yolo-Based Machine Learning Algorithms in the Municipality of Los Baños, https://doi.org/10.1109/icaisg68699.2025.11452147]. Mathematical deduction applied to these reported figures indicates that the majority of these false positives originate from the parking class. Without temporal continuity or contextual awareness of surrounding traffic dynamics, single-frame spatial co-occurrence models lack a mechanism to distinguish between a vehicle parked next to a no-parking sign and a vehicle that has momentarily stopped in traffic next to the same sign.

Researchers working with mobile camera platforms (dashcams and crowdsensing smartphones) have explored the collective behavior and spatial relationships of surrounding vehicles to infer the ambient traffic state [iPatrol: Illegal Roadside Parking Detection Leveraging On-road Vehicle Crowdsensing, https://doi.org/10.1109/iwqos61813.2024.10682887], [When Vehicle Crowd-Sensing Meets Complex Driving: Robust Illegal Roadside Parking Detections, https://doi.org/10.1109/tmc.2026.3665356], [Deep Learning-Based Stopped Vehicle Detection Method Utilizing In-Vehicle Dashcams, https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics13204097]. These mobile methodologies demonstrate, in principle, that surrounding vehicle motion serves as a useful contextual signal for distinguishing legitimate stops from violations. However, they analyze surrounding traffic primarily to compensate for ego-motion uncertainty inherent to moving cameras and rely on hardcoded mathematical distance thresholds rather than machine-learned decision boundaries.

Fixed-camera systems occupy a structurally advantageous position relative to mobile platforms: they produce stable ground-plane coordinates that can be calibrated using 2D homography [Application of 2D Homography for High Resolution Traffic Data Collection using CCTV Cameras, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07220], and they generate surrounding motion data continuously as a byproduct of multi-object tracking [High-speed tracking-by-detection without using image information, https://doi.org/10.1109/AVSS.2017.8078516]. Despite this, among the fixed-camera frameworks reviewed, none utilize the aggregate motion state of surrounding tracked vehicles as a machine-learned decision input for violation classification. The contextual motion data that the tracker already computes for each surrounding vehicle is discarded after the tracking phase rather than forwarded to the decision logic. The utility of surrounding vehicle context has been validated in the mobile-platform literature [iPatrol: Illegal Roadside Parking Detection Leveraging On-road Vehicle Crowdsensing, https://doi.org/10.1109/iwqos61813.2024.10682887], [When Vehicle Crowd-Sensing Meets Complex Driving: Robust Illegal Roadside Parking Detections, https://doi.org/10.1109/tmc.2026.3665356], [Deep Learning-Based Stopped Vehicle Detection Method Utilizing In-Vehicle Dashcams, https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics13204097], yet this principle has not been transferred to fixed-camera frameworks, where the geometric stability of a stationary camera eliminates the ego-motion confound that motivates the mobile approaches.

## Statement of the Problem

Based on the limitations identified in existing computer vision-based illegal parking detection frameworks, this study seeks to answer the following research questions:

- How effectively can the aggregate spatiotemporal motion data of surrounding tracked vehicles, specifically modeled through a 6-dimensional feature vector ($R, C, \bar{d}, \sigma_d, \Delta R, d_{\text{cand}}$), serve as a decision input for a trained classifier to differentiate genuine illegal parking from legitimate collective traffic stops, without relying on fixed external infrastructure?
- How does the proposed context-aware spatiotemporal classifier perform compared to a single-frame spatial co-occurrence baseline [Smart Surveillance of Illegal Parking and Littering Detection Using Yolo-Based Machine Learning Algorithms in the Municipality of Los Baños, https://doi.org/10.1109/icaisg68699.2025.11452147] in terms of precision, recall, F1-score, and false positive reduction?

## Objectives

The objectives of the study are as follows:

- To develop a computer vision pipeline that integrates an XGBoost classifier, trained on a 6-dimensional spatiotemporal feature vector, to differentiate genuine illegal parking violations from legitimate collective traffic stops without reliance on fixed external infrastructure.
- To evaluate and compare the performance of the proposed context-aware classifier against a single-frame spatial co-occurrence baseline [Smart Surveillance of Illegal Parking and Littering Detection Using Yolo-Based Machine Learning Algorithms in the Municipality of Los Baños, https://doi.org/10.1109/icaisg68699.2025.11452147], specifically measuring precision, recall, F1-score, and the reduction of traffic-induced false positives.

## Significance of the Study

This study is significant for law enforcement agencies and local government units, because automated monitoring frameworks reduce the need for continuous manual surveillance of restricted zones [A Machine Vision Detection of Unauthorized On-Street Roadside Parking in Restricted Zone: an Experimental Simulated Barangay-Environment, https://doi.org/10.30534/ijeter/2020/17842020], [Parking Violation Detection Using Computer Vision for Urban Traffic Management, https://doi.org/10.1109/iciss63372.2025.11076537]. The primary objective of these automated deployments is to provide highly reliable, objective identification of genuine infractions [Computer Vision and Deep Learning Based Approach for Violations Due to Illegal Parking Detection, https://doi.org/10.5120/ijca2025924506], [An Automated Classification of Vehicles and Violation Detection in Special Purpose Lanes, https://doi.org/10.1109/tensymp55890.2023.10223478]. However, because standard systems frequently generate false positive alerts during traffic congestion, human operators are forced to expend time manually reviewing and dismissing invalid alerts. By validating a context-aware classifier that mechanically filters these legitimate stops, this study minimizes administrative overhead, establishing a more robust technical baseline that allows enforcement personnel to allocate their resources efficiently [Computer Vision and Deep Learning Based Approach for Violations Due to Illegal Parking Detection, https://doi.org/10.5120/ijca2025924506].

For urban planners and policymakers, the study offers a practical solution that conforms to the constraints of edge-AI enabled video analytics [Opportunities, Applications, and Challenges of Edge-AI Enabled Video Analytics in Smart Cities: A Systematic Review, https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3300658]. It does not require expensive supplementary sensors, heavy end-to-end deep neural networks, or traffic signal API feeds. The framework is designed to operate on fixed cameras already installed in urban areas, making it a highly cost-effective option for managing curb space [A New Curb Lane Monitoring and Illegal Parking Impact Estimation Approach Based on Queueing Theory and Computer Vision for Cameras with Low Resolution and Low Frame Rate, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2022.05.024], [Computer Vision and Deep Learning Based Approach for Violations Due to Illegal Parking Detection, https://doi.org/10.5120/ijca2025924506]. It supports the goals of SDG 9 and SDG 11 by assisting in the creation of organized urban environments, while aligning with the Philippine Development Plan 2023-2028 and AmBisyon Natin 2040 [ASSESSING THE IMPACT OF "PARKING NG BAYAN" IN REGULATING VEHICLE PARKING IN PUBLIC AREAS OF VALENZUELA CITY, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15706773].

The general public benefits from the reduction of illegally parked vehicles, which block roads and emergency routes, increasing the probability of rear-end collisions [City Scale Monitoring of On-Street Parking Violations with StreetHAWK, https://doi.org/10.1145/3360322.3360841]. A system that effectively enforces parking restrictions reduces cruising time, which consequently lowers fuel consumption and urban emissions [ASSESSING THE IMPACT OF "PARKING NG BAYAN" IN REGULATING VEHICLE PARKING IN PUBLIC AREAS OF VALENZUELA CITY, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15706773], [On-street Illegal Parking Costs in Urban Areas, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sbspro.2014.12.146].

Finally, this study contributes to the academic community by directly addressing a documented structural limitation in vision-based violation decision logic. The literature demonstrates that evaluating candidate vehicles in strict isolation inherently produces false positive detections during collective traffic stops. By applying traffic flow theory to computer vision and validating a spatiotemporal classification framework that utilizes the kinematic state of surrounding traffic, this research elevates the theoretical and technical foundation for complex behavior analysis in fixed-camera environments.

## Scope and Limitations of the Study

This study focuses solely on fixed-camera, computer vision-based frameworks for detecting illegal parking. It does not include mobile camera platforms, dashcams, UAVs, or crowdsensing systems [When Vehicle Crowd-Sensing Meets Complex Driving: Robust Illegal Roadside Parking Detections, https://doi.org/10.1109/tmc.2026.3665356], [UAV-Based Intelligent Traffic Surveillance System: Real-Time Vehicle Detection, Classification, Tracking, and Behavioral Analysis, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04624v1], [Deep Learning-Based Stopped Vehicle Detection Method Utilizing In-Vehicle Dashcams, https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics13204097]. The architecture utilizes video footage from a single camera and does not integrate external hardware, additional sensors, or traffic signal API feeds.

The contribution of this study is strictly restricted to the violation decision logic phase. The vision pipeline utilizes pretrained YOLOv8 weights [Parking Time Violation Tracking Using YOLOv8 and Tracking Algorithms, https://doi.org/10.3390/s23135843] and a standard IoU-based multi-object tracker [Enhanced Detection of Illegally Parked Vehicles Using YOLO and Good Feature to Track Methods, https://doi.org/10.1109/apsipaasc63619.2025.10849019], [High-speed tracking-by-detection without using image information, https://doi.org/10.1109/AVSS.2017.8078516]. The deployment constraint of real-time edge processing [Opportunities, Applications, and Challenges of Edge-AI Enabled Video Analytics in Smart Cities: A Systematic Review, https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3300658] dictates that the violation logic must incur minimal computational overhead; therefore, an XGBoost classifier is utilized over computationally heavy end-to-end deep neural networks. The study does not propose new object detection architectures or resolve upstream tracking identity switches.

The empirical evaluation of this study is geographically and structurally bounded to a single elevated fixed-camera vantage point overlooking an unsignalized urban roadway corridor [test site location to be specified upon site confirmation]. The research team collects a primary video dataset at the selected location, selecting a site with urban traffic density and road geometry comparable to typical on-street surveillance environments [Smart Surveillance of Illegal Parking and Littering Detection Using Yolo-Based Machine Learning Algorithms in the Municipality of Los Baños, https://doi.org/10.1109/icaisg68699.2025.11452147]. The restricted zone (Region of Interest) is defined as a static closed polygon spatially grounded in the statutory provisions of Republic Act No. 4136 (Land Transportation and Traffic Code of the Philippines). The framework does not claim universal, zero-shot deployment across arbitrary uncalibrated locations; camera intrinsic parameters and 2D ground-plane homography calibration [Application of 2D Homography for High Resolution Traffic Data Collection using CCTV Cameras, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07220] are specific to the physical camera mounting height, tilt angle, and site geometry of the designated test location.

Data acquisition is strictly confined to daytime peak traffic hours (16:00 to 18:00) under clear or overcast weather conditions to ensure adequate surrounding vehicle density for contextual feature extraction. Adverse atmospheric conditions, such as severe precipitation, and low-illumination nighttime scenarios are explicitly excluded from the scope, as visual noise and illumination degradation compromise the tracking stability of lightweight bounding box detectors [Illegal Parking Detection Using Gaussian Mixture Model and Kalman Filter, https://doi.org/10.1109/aiccsa.2017.212], [Parking Time Violation Tracking Using YOLOv8 and Tracking Algorithms, https://doi.org/10.3390/s23135843].

Evaluation is limited to candidate vehicles that have remained stationary inside the restricted zone for a minimum temporal threshold of 60 seconds, consistent with standard ITS literature [Parking Violation Detection Using Computer Vision for Urban Traffic Management, https://doi.org/10.1109/iciss63372.2025.11076537], [Detecting illegally parked vehicle based on cumulative dual foreground difference, https://doi.org/10.1109/indin.2016.7819263]. Consequently, stationary events lasting less than 60 seconds are structurally excluded from the evaluation. 

The context-aware classifier explicitly requires the presence of at least two tracked vehicles in the scene to compute a mathematically valid contextual feature vector. If a candidate vehicle is completely isolated with no surrounding traffic, the system bypasses the classifier and gracefully degrades to standard temporal threshold logic. This fallback is highly optimal: in isolated scenarios characterized by extremely low traffic volume, standard timer-only logic performs perfectly because there are no collective traffic stops to misclassify. The context-aware classifier specifically targets the high-density urban scenarios where standard systems fail most severely.

The baseline method [Smart Surveillance of Illegal Parking and Littering Detection Using Yolo-Based Machine Learning Algorithms in the Municipality of Los Baños, https://doi.org/10.1109/icaisg68699.2025.11452147] is evaluated via a same-dataset reimplementation under identical test conditions. Because the proposed method defines a violation based on continuous dwell-time, whereas the baseline method defines a violation based on single-frame spatial co-occurrence, a structural distinction in the ground truth formulation exists. The comparative evaluation strictly tests discrimination ability within the pool of extended-duration stationary events. Validating the classifier's generalization capabilities across varying intersection geometries and highly divergent traffic patterns is identified as an area for future research.
